# Computational Physics
***Technical Notes***  
**Author:** Rodrigo Kang

This directory contains systematic notes on computational physics, with an emphasis on the mathematical and numerical methods used to model, analyse, and simulate physical systems.

The notes develop the underlying physical and mathematical principles alongside their computational treatment, connecting theoretical models with numerical methods, implementation, and interpretation of results.

## Scope

The material may cover topics such as numerical solutions of differential equations, dynamical systems, classical mechanics, statistical physics, quantum mechanics, field theories, numerical linear algebra, and simulation methods.

Particular attention is given to the formulation of physical problems in a form suitable for computation, the selection and analysis of numerical methods, and the relationship between mathematical approximations and the physical systems they represent.

## Approach

The notes are developed from first principles where this helps clarify the physical assumptions, mathematical structure, and computational formulation of a problem. Analytical results are used whenever possible to provide insight, establish reference solutions, or guide the construction and validation of numerical methods.

The emphasis is not only on obtaining numerical results, but also on understanding:

* the physical assumptions underlying a model;
* how the governing equations are derived;
* how continuous models are translated into computational problems;
* the numerical methods used to solve or simulate them;
* the accuracy, stability, and computational cost of those methods;
* how numerical results should be validated and interpreted; and
* where the physical or numerical approximations cease to be reliable.
