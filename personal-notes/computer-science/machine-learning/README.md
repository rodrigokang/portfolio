# Machine Learning
**Technical Notes**
**Author:** Rodrigo Kang

This directory contains systematic notes on machine learning, with an emphasis on the mathematical, statistical, and computational principles underlying learning algorithms and their application to data-driven problems.

The notes develop methods from their theoretical foundations through to their computational implementation, with particular attention to model assumptions, optimisation, statistical interpretation, generalisation, and practical behaviour.

## Scope

The material is organised around two broad areas: **classical machine learning** and **quantum machine learning**.

Classical machine learning covers the foundations and methods of statistical and computational learning, including supervised and unsupervised learning, regression and classification, dimensionality reduction, ensemble methods, neural networks, model evaluation, and related approaches.

Quantum machine learning considers learning methods that incorporate concepts and computational models from quantum mechanics and quantum computing, including quantum representations of data, parameterised quantum models, hybrid quantum-classical methods, and quantum approaches to learning and optimisation.

The scope may extend from fundamental principles and established algorithms to more specialised methods and contemporary developments in both areas.

## Approach

The notes are developed from first principles where this helps clarify why a method works, the assumptions on which it relies, and the mathematical structure of the underlying learning problem. Analytical derivations are complemented by computational implementations and numerical experiments where appropriate.

The emphasis is not only on applying machine learning algorithms, but also on understanding:

* how learning problems are formulated mathematically;
* the assumptions underlying models and algorithms;
* how objective functions and learning procedures are derived;
* how models are trained and optimised;
* how generalisation and predictive performance are evaluated;
* the computational properties and limitations of different methods;
* how theoretical results relate to observed model behaviour; and
* when a particular modelling approach is appropriate for the problem being considered.
